# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack
Existing: plain static HTML/CSS/vanilla JS in `frontend/` (no build step). Keep it that way.

## Users
First-year Dartmouth students before and during the involvement fair: overwhelmed, new to campus, deciding which clubs to try this term. Returning students exploring something new are a secondary audience.

## Product Purpose
Help a student find clubs that fit them: search the list, enter a profile (classes, current clubs, a short description of themselves), and get recommendations in three lanes: serious clubs that match their interests, fun clubs to try something new, and random clubs. Success: a student leaves with a short, realistic shortlist they feel good about.

## Positioning
Unlike a flat directory, it explains why each club fits (from the student's own classes and interests), is upfront about time commitment, cost, and how to join (open, application, audition, deadline), and offers three discovery lanes: grow, play, surprise.

## Operating Context
Used on a phone or laptop around the involvement fair, often standing in line or in a dorm. Profile and shortlist live only in the browser (localStorage); no accounts, no backend. A printable fair checklist and a shareable shortlist link exist.

## Capabilities and Constraints
Browse/search/filter clubs; profile with clickable class list, interests, current clubs, optional class times (schedule-conflict flags); recommendations with reasons and feedback; shortlist with weekly-hours meter and deadlines; club detail dialog with deep links. All club and course data is placeholder/sample; real data is not yet available. Must stay a static site.

## Brand Commitments
Must feel Dartmouth (user-confirmed): keep a Dartmouth-green, collegiate character. Not an official Dartmouth product; do not use official logos or imply endorsement.

## Evidence on Hand
No real club data, testimonials, or user research. Do not fabricate club facts or claims; sample-data disclosure must remain visible.

## Product Principles
1. Explain every recommendation; trust comes from visible reasons.
2. Be honest about cost: time, money, and how hard it is to get in.
3. Make a shortlist, not a spreadsheet: reduce overwhelm, keep choices few.
4. Curiosity over optimization: the "try something new" and "random" lanes are first-class.
5. Works for a nervous first-year on a phone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Keyboard and screen-reader usable, WCAG AA contrast in light and dark, reduced-motion respected.
